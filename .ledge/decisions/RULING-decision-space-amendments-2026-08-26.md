## 2. The Eight Operative Rulings

### R-A2 — DELEGATION IS PER-GRANT, NEVER STANDING

**No grant survives its wave.** Authority is issued per grant, consumed within
the wave it was issued for, and lapses there. No standing delegation, no rollover.

*Cost accepted:* re-granting friction each wave — one operator word per wave, not once.

### R-A3 — GRANT GRAMMAR: DEFINED TIERS, INVOKED BY NAME

Two named tiers; a grant is expressed as **tier name + outcome**.

**USER-GRADE** — config levers already ruled · replays via proven lanes · paper/code
authorship, PRs, merges-on-green · seat/rite invocations · all non-prod acts.
**ADMIN-GRADE** — all of USER-GRADE, **plus**: environment-gate deploy approvals ·
service rolls/redeploys · reversible business-ledger **METADATA** writes (the unstamp
class; **never record content**) · delegated enactment of operator-named surfaces
(prominent disclosure, operator-amendable). Novel act-classes are **not** admitted
by analogy: they require a tier amendment or an explicit named extension in the
grant itself.

*Cost accepted:* a novel act-class stalls at the tier boundary until named.

### R-A4 — NEVER-GRANTABLE FLOOR

**No wording reaches these**, in any grant, at any tier: (1) credential-rotation
**EXECUTION**; (2) customer-visible **outbound** acts; (3) business-of-record
**identity mints**. The floor sits **above both tiers** — ADMIN-GRADE does not
approach it, and no grant phrasing, however explicit, lifts it.

*Cost accepted:* the floor blocks even a well-argued, obviously-safe instance.
